The machine

Every collection worth visiting has one exhibit whose label ends in a question mark. This is ours. The machine is plainly built on Greenlee’s 495 variety-saw pattern, but it is not a standard 495 — the evidence points to a special-order model, most likely a sliding-table variant, in which a rolling carriage carries the work past the blade for square, repeatable crosscuts on wide panels. Factories ordered such machines against specific product lines, which makes each one a small mystery: to identify the machine is to identify the work it was built for.

This example

The 495s is in the research stage: serial number, build date, and exact configuration are all being traced through casting marks and period catalogs. Its page will be rewritten as the answers come in — museums earn trust by admitting what they do not yet know, and this collection intends to earn it. Restoration will follow the paperwork.
